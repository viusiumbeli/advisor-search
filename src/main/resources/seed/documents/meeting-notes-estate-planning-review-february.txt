MEETING NOTES: ESTATE PLANNING REVIEW

Client: Mrs Deborah Feinstein (client reference CW-0883)
Date and place: 11 February 2026, at the client's home in Radlett
Present: Mrs Feinstein; Priya Raghunathan, chartered financial planner, Copperbeech Wealth Planning
Duration: approximately 100 minutes

Background

Deborah is 74, widowed since the death of her husband Gerald on 4 March 2019. Gerald left his entire estate to her, so both his nil rate band and his residence nil rate band are fully transferable. She has two children, Miriam and David, and four grandchildren: Eli (15), Hannah (12), Noah (9) and Rivka (6). She is also a trustee of the Feinstein Family Educational Trust, which was not discussed in detail today and is outside the scope of this review.

Estate value and inheritance tax exposure

Current values: main residence £925,000, investment portfolio £1,140,000, cash deposits £185,000, chattels and jewellery £90,000. Total estate £2,340,000. Her own nil rate band of £325,000 plus Gerald's transferred band gives £650,000. The combined residence nil rate band would be £350,000, but it tapers by £1 for every £2 the estate exceeds £2,000,000: the £340,000 excess reduces it by £170,000, leaving £180,000 available. Total bands £830,000, leaving £1,510,000 exposed and an inheritance tax liability today of approximately £604,000. I stressed that reducing the estate below £2,000,000 restores residence nil rate band at an effective 60 per cent marginal saving in the taper zone, which makes lifetime giving unusually efficient for her.

Gifts already made

Two potentially exempt transfers are in progress. £50,000 to Miriam in June 2021 falls outside the estate from June 2028. £20,000 towards David's house deposit in March 2024 falls out in March 2031. I explained that taper relief reduces the tax, not the value, and only applies to gifts above the nil rate band, so neither gift would attract taper relief if she died tomorrow; they would simply use up part of the band. Annual exemptions of £3,000 have been used each tax year since 2023/24.

Surplus income exemption

Her net income is around £68,000 a year from pensions and dividends against expenditure of roughly £41,000, a surplus of about £27,000. Gifts that are regular, made from income, and leave her able to maintain her normal standard of living are exempt immediately, with no seven-year clock. The exemption stands or falls on records: her executors would need a year-by-year schedule of income, expenditure and gifts in the format of HMRC form IHT403. We discussed a pattern of £2,000 a month into the grandchildren's junior ISAs, evidenced by a letter of intent to establish regularity from the first payment.

Discretionary trust for the grandchildren

We discussed settling £250,000 into a discretionary trust. As a chargeable lifetime transfer within her own unused £325,000 nil rate band there would be no entry charge; ten-yearly charges of up to 6 per cent would apply thereafter. The structure answers her concern about the grandchildren receiving capital at 18, since the trustees control timing and amounts. Deborah was candid that her real hesitation is broader: "I am not ready to hand over money I might need if I end up needing care." I confirmed we would not recommend giving away more than the cashflow model shows she can spare with a prudent care-cost assumption, and the decision was deferred to the next meeting.

Existing will

Her will is dated 9 May 2011, prepared by a firm that has since closed. Its shortcomings: the sole executor named is her brother Saul, who died in 2023; everything passes outright and equally to Miriam and David, with no provision for the grandchildren; there is no letter of wishes; and it predates the residence nil rate band, so nothing in it was drafted with that relief in mind. It needs replacing rather than amending.

Agreed actions

1. Priya Raghunathan to prepare the surplus income and expenditure schedule and a gifting record template by 6 March 2026.
2. Deborah to instruct Marlowe and Tance LLP to prepare a new will by 31 March 2026; Priya to send the solicitor a briefing note within one week.
3. Priya to build a cashflow model comparing the £250,000 discretionary trust against phased outright gifts, including a care-cost stress test, for the next meeting.
4. Deborah to locate the paperwork evidencing the 2021 and 2024 gifts and pass copies to us.
5. Next meeting: 22 April 2026, 10.30 am, Copperbeech offices.
