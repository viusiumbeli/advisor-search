Covering Note and Summary
Registered Lasting Power of Attorney - Property and Financial Affairs
Donor: Mr Samuel Ashworth-Bell

Prepared by Marchbanks Private Client Law, Norwich, for the donor's family and for any bank, building society or investment manager asked to act on the attorneys' instructions. This note summarises the registered instrument; it does not replace it. Certified copies of the full instrument are available from this firm on request.

The donor

Samuel Ashworth-Bell, born 2 April 1951, is a retired educator who spent thirty-one years teaching history and latterly served as deputy head of a Norwich sixth-form college. He lives at 6 Weaver's Yard, Norwich NR3 1EJ. He made this lasting power of attorney while in good health, on the advice of this firm, so that his financial affairs can be managed without interruption should he one day become unable to manage them himself.

The attorneys

The donor appointed two attorneys: his daughter, Dr Imogen Ashworth-Bell of Cambridge, and his long-standing friend and former colleague, Mr Rajesh Chandrasekaran of Norwich. They are appointed jointly and severally, which means that either may act alone and each may sign documents without the other, although the donor has asked them in his accompanying letter to consult one another on any decision involving more than £5,000.

If either attorney dies, loses capacity or becomes unable or unwilling to act, the donor named a replacement attorney: his niece, Mrs Catherine Voss of Wymondham, who would step into the vacant appointment on the same joint and several basis.

When the power can be used

The donor chose the option allowing the attorneys to act as soon as the instrument was registered, including while he retains capacity, provided he consents. In practice he continues to manage his own affairs, and the attorneys act only on his specific request, as they did when he wintered abroad in late 2025. If he loses the ability to make financial decisions, their authority continues without any further formality.

Certificate provider and registration

Dr Helen Mowbray, the donor's general practitioner of eleven years, acted as certificate provider, confirming on 14 October 2025 that the donor understood the instrument and was under no pressure to make it. The instrument was registered by the Office of the Public Guardian on 9 January 2026 under OPG reference 7XZQ-0413-8876. The registration stamp appears on every page of the original.

The attorneys' duties

The attorneys are bound by the Mental Capacity Act 2005 and its Code of Practice. They must act in the donor's best interests at all times, help him make his own decisions wherever possible, and take the least restrictive course available. They must keep his money and property entirely separate from their own, maintain accounts and records of all dealings, and be ready to produce them to the Office of the Public Guardian on request. They may recover reasonable out-of-pocket expenses but receive no payment for acting.

Restrictions on gifts

The donor imposed a written restriction: the attorneys may make gifts only on customary occasions such as birthdays, weddings and religious festivals, and only to people connected with him. No single gift may exceed £250, and total gifts in any tax year are capped at £2,000. Gifts to the attorneys themselves are permitted within those limits only. Anything beyond them requires an application to the Court of Protection.

Notifying banks and investment managers

Each institution holding the donor's accounts or investments should be sent a certified copy of the registered instrument, or given the access code issued by the Office of the Public Guardian for its online view service, together with specimen signatures for both attorneys. The donor holds current and deposit accounts with two banks and a discretionary portfolio with an investment manager; this firm keeps the current list.

If the donor loses capacity

The attorneys should first obtain a written medical opinion confirming the position, then notify this firm and every institution on the list, take over routine payments and household outgoings, and review the donor's investments with his portfolio manager to ensure the arrangements still serve his needs. They must keep full records throughout and continue to involve the donor in decisions so far as he is able to participate.
